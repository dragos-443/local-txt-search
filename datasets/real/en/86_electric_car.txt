An electric car is an automobile that uses electrical energy as the primary source of propulsion. The term normally refers to a plug-in electric vehicle, typically a battery electric vehicle (BEV), which only uses energy stored in on-board battery packs, but broadly may also include plug-in hybrid electric vehicle (PHEV), range-extended electric vehicle (REEV) and fuel cell electric vehicle (FCEV), which can convert electric power from other fuels via a generator or a fuel cell.

Compared to conventional internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicles, electric cars are quieter, more responsive, have superior energy conversion efficiency and produce no tailpipe emissions. Electric cars generally have a lower overall carbon footprint over their life cycle, from manufacturing to end of life, even when the electricity used for charging comes partly from fossil-fuel power stations. Electric cars generally have higher manufacturing emissions than comparable ICE vehicles, although they produce fewer greenhouse gas emissions during operation. Due to the superior efficiency of electric motors, electric cars also generate less waste heat, thus reducing the need for engine cooling systems that are often large, complicated and maintenance-prone in ICE vehicles. However, EVs may still use dedicated thermal management systems to regulate battery temperature.

The battery of a plug-in electric car is typically recharged by connecting it to an external electricity supply. Recharging an electric car can be done at different kinds of charging stations; depending on local regulations and utility requirements, these charging stations can be installed in private homes, parking garages and public areas. There is also research and development in, as well as deployment of, other technologies such as battery swapping and inductive charging. As charging stations (especially fast chargers) become more widely available, range anxiety and the time required to charge remain psychological barriers for some consumers considering the purchase of an electric car. Governments and electric vehicle manufacturers have introduced various initiatives to expand charging infrastructure, improve charging accessibility and address these concerns.

Worldwide, 21 million plug-in electric cars were sold in 2025, accounting for one-quarter of new car sales and representing an increase of more than 20% compared with 2024. Many countries have established government incentives for plug-in electric vehicles, including tax credits, subsidies, and other non-monetary incentives while several countries have legislated to phase-out of fossil fuel vehicles, to reduce air pollution and limit climate change.

China has the world's largest stock of new energy vehicles, with 31.4 million vehicles in use (vehicle ownership) at the end of 2024, including 22.09 million battery electric vehicles (BEVs). These figures also include heavy-duty commercial vehicles such as buses, garbage trucks and sanitation vehicles, and only account for vehicles manufactured in China. As of 2020, the total cost of ownership of recent electric vehicles in the United States and European Union was lower than that of equivalent ICE cars, due to lower power and maintenance costs.

In 2023, the Tesla Model Y became the world's best-selling car. The Tesla Model 3 became the world's all-time best-selling electric car in early 2020, and in June 2021 it became the first electric car to pass 1 million global sales.
